## Consistency notes from the screenshots

- The four product photos show four different objects: a sage pedestal lick bowl, a dotted-texture bowl with a snuffle mat, pastel suction bowls with spoons, and a plastic puzzle feeder. The copy describes one "elevated wobble bowl" in "one-piece silicone". The ad landing page must show one consistent product, photographed for real.
- The "Textured Silicone" tile shows ceramic-look bowls of kibble, and the "Recipes" tile shows a ridged feeding bowl. Neither is the lick bowl. Replace with shots of the actual product.
- The footer bar says PHILIPPINES ₱ PHP while the product grid prices are in AUD. One currency must win.
- The Enrichment Initiative carries a ® symbol. Do not show a registered mark unless the mark is actually registered.
- The feature icons (bag, envelope, tag, calendar) are template leftovers and do not describe the features.
- Video block runtime is 10 seconds. Fine for a demonstration loop if it shows the routine end to end.
- Photography style to carry forward: warm floors, white walls, real dogs mid-action, product low in frame. The hero is the strongest image on the page.

## What holds it back as a paid-traffic landing page

1. **No customer problem.** The page starts selling before it names the situation the buyer recognises (bath-time wrestling, nail-trim panic, a dog that bolts food, a bored dog home alone). The statement in section 6 has the idea but buries it two screens down.
2. **Catalogue before conviction.** Four products with prices appear immediately after the hero, before any benefit. That is a store home page pattern, not a conversion page. For ads, one product and one offer should lead; the bundle is the upsell.
3. **Two lifestyle trios say the same thing.** Sections 3 and 5 both show grooming and enrichment tiles. Merge into one demonstration sequence.
4. **Claims without support.** "Industrial suction", "locks firmly to tile, glass, or polished tubs", "BPA-free food-grade silicone", "dishwasher", "30-Day Guarantee", "Grooming Distraction Studies", and the shelter initiative all need either supplier or policy confirmation, or softer wording, before ads run.
5. **No objection handling.** No comparison against a flat lick mat or a standard bowl, no FAQ, no sizing or suitability guidance (dog size, chewers, supervision).
6. **No proof.** The nav promises REVIEWS, the footer links Customer Reviews, but no review section exists. Better an honest placeholder than a dead link.
7. **CTA drift.** SHOP THE BOWL, LEARN MORE, JOIN, and a product grid all compete. On a landing page every primary CTA should go to the one product page.
8. **Market signals conflict.** Stockists are in the Philippines and Singapore, prices are in AUD, and the studio is in Davao. Decide the launch market and currency for the ad landing page.
9. **Mobile.** The generated layout is desktop-first; the 4-up grids and split panels need a defined stacking order and a sticky CTA.
10. **No tracking or funnel plan** from ad to Shopify product page.

## How the improved version will be built

Same discipline as the cat fountain mock, applied to this product:

1. Hero that continues the ad angle (bath-time / boredom / fast-eater), one CTA to the exact Shopify product.
2. Problem section in the owner's words.
3. Product as the answer, three benefit pillars: elevated wobble, suction hold, freeze-and-lick.
4. Demonstration: the 18-second video plus a numbered routine (fill, freeze, stick, distract, rinse).
5. Feature grid using the four existing features with claim-safe wording.
6. Where it goes: bath, grooming table, kitchen floor, crate.
7. Cleaning and care.
8. Comparison: flat lick mat vs standard bowl vs elevated bowl.
9. What's included.
10. Offer: single bowl vs bundle, bundle preferred.
11. Reviews placeholder until real ones exist.
12. FAQ: suction surfaces, dog sizes, chewers, dishwasher, freezing, supervision.
13. Final CTA, Davao studio story and initiative as trust, footer.
